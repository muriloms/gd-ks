# 📚 Story Bible - Workflow Instructions

## Overview

This workflow guides you through creating a comprehensive Story Bible - the definitive document that captures your game's complete narrative foundation. By the end, you'll have a detailed reference for all story-related decisions.

**Time:** 60-90 minutes  
**Difficulty:** Intermediate  
**Output:** `story-bible.md`

---

## Step 1: Story Overview

### The Foundation

Let's establish what your story IS at its core.

#### Story Premise

Complete this template:
```
In a world where [SETTING/SITUATION],
[PROTAGONIST] must [GOAL/ACTION]
before [STAKES/DEADLINE],
but [OBSTACLE/ANTAGONIST] stands in the way.
```

#### Story Questions

Answer these foundational questions:

1. **What is this story ABOUT?** (Theme, not plot)
   - Example: "It's about redemption" vs "It's about a soldier who..."

2. **What HAPPENS?** (Plot in one sentence)
   - Beginning → Middle → End

3. **Why should players CARE?**
   - Emotional hook
   - Relatable element

4. **What's the TONE?**
   - Light/Dark
   - Serious/Comedic
   - Hopeful/Grim
   - Realistic/Fantastical

## Step 2: Themes & Messages

### What Your Story Explores

Great games say something meaningful. What does yours say?

#### Primary Theme

```yaml
Theme: [One word or phrase]
Statement: [What the story says about this theme]
How It's Explored: [Through what elements]

Example:
  Theme: Sacrifice
  Statement: "True sacrifice requires giving up what you love, not what you hate"
  How It's Explored: 
    - Player choices that cost something valuable
    - NPCs who sacrifice for different reasons
    - Final choice that embodies the theme
```

#### Secondary Themes

List 2-3 supporting themes:
- Theme: [Name] → Explored through [Element]
- Theme: [Name] → Explored through [Element]

#### Motifs & Symbols

Recurring elements that reinforce themes:

| [Color] | [Represents] | [Where it appears] |
| [Sound/Music] | [Represents] | [Where it appears] |

## Step 3: Story Structure

### Narrative Arc

Choose and map your structure:

#### Three-Act Structure

```
ACT 1 - SETUP (25%)
├── Opening Image: [First impression]
├── Ordinary World: [Status quo]
├── Inciting Incident: [What changes everything]
├── Debate: [Should they accept the call?]
└── Break into Act 2: [Point of no return]

ACT 2 - CONFRONTATION (50%)
├── B Story: [Subplot/relationship]
├── Fun & Games: [Promise of the premise]
├── Midpoint: [Stakes raise, false victory/defeat]
├── Bad Guys Close In: [Pressure mounts]
├── All Is Lost: [Lowest point]
└── Dark Night of Soul: [Moment of despair]

ACT 3 - RESOLUTION (25%)
├── Break into Act 3: [New plan/resolve]
├── Finale: [Climax confrontation]
├── Final Image: [New status quo]
└── Denouement: [Aftermath]
```

#### Key Story Beats

| Beat | Description | Gameplay Connection |
|------|-------------|---------------------|
| Hook | [What grabs attention] | [How player experiences] |
| Lock-In | [Commitment point] | [Player choice/action] |
| Midpoint | [Everything changes] | [Major reveal/event] |
| Climax | [Final confrontation] | [Boss/challenge] |
| Resolution | [New equilibrium] | [Ending sequence] |

## Step 4: Central Conflicts

### The Engine of Your Story

Every story needs conflict to drive it forward.

#### External Conflict


## Tips for a Great Story Bible

✅ **DO:**
- Keep it updated as the game evolves
- Make it searchable and organized
- Include visual references where helpful
- Note what's CANON vs what's flexible
- Consider how gameplay tells story

❌ **DON'T:**
- Write a novel (be concise)
- Lock yourself into unmalleable details
- Forget that players might miss things
- Ignore ludonarrative dissonance
- Separate story from gameplay entirely
